OUR STORY

The first lessons came from Madam Grace.

This is the founder’s formative entrepreneurship story: a childhood experience of commerce, responsibility, customers, sourcing and movement of goods.

MADAM GRACE

Kabiama · Sagbama LGA · Bayelsa

TRADE & DISTRIBUTION

From local sourcing to bigger markets.

THE FORMATIVE YEARS

Learning business by doing the work.

Madam Grace was a well-known trader and entrepreneur in Kabiama, Sagbama LGA. Her business connected farmers, commodities and markets.

01 · SOURCING

Closer to the producer

She sourced commodities directly from farmers and aggregated goods for trade, building a practical understanding of where products came from and how supply moved.

02 · STORE

A young Jerry behind the counter

When Madam Grace travelled to Aba to restock the business, Jerry often remained in the store, helping with sales, attending to customers and keeping the day-to-day business moving.

03 · MARKET

Learning through real transactions

He also accompanied her on larger-market trips. Through those experiences he learned about purchasing, pricing, storage, customer demand and distribution.

04 · MEMORY

Grace — later known also as Onoriode

For the story on this site, we introduce her respectfully as Madam Grace, and later bring in her other/native name, Onoriode, as part of the chronology.

“Find value, understand the market, manage resources carefully, serve people well, and build for growth.” — the business principle this experience helped shape.
FROM EXPERIENCE TO HONEYGUIDE

The lesson became a direction.

The journey did not stop at the shop. Later education, industrial work and entrepreneurship built on that early exposure to responsibility and commerce.

LATER

Education & industrial experience

Jerry’s documented journey includes university education, industrial project work, HSE training and entrepreneurial development. These experiences sit alongside the early lessons from commerce.

TODAY

HoneyGuide Group Limited

That foundation now informs a company focused on agricultural value addition, food manufacturing, innovation and practical market-building.
