THE FOUNDER'S JOURNEY

From humble beginnings to HoneyGuide.

This story is not presented as a claim that one person already knows everything required to build a food company. It explains the documented experiences, responsibilities and practical lessons that shaped the founder—and the discipline with which those experiences are being applied to HoneyGuide.

ORIGIN · AGRICULTURE · VALUE

Opportunity is only the beginning. Value is created by what you do with it.

MADAM GRACE

The first practical education in enterprise.

HoneyGuide's story begins before the company had a name. It begins with the founder's exposure, as a young lad, to the commercial activities of his late grandmother, Madam Grace—later introduced in the family story by her native name, Onoriode.

Madam Grace was a respected trader and entrepreneur in Kabiama, Sagbama LGA, Bayelsa State, with business activities connected to agricultural commodities and surrounding communities. Her approach was practical: identify what people produced, understand where it could be obtained, aggregate useful quantities, preserve or process what needed handling, and connect the goods with buyers in larger markets.

Her commercial activities covered commodities such as ogbono, palm oil, snails, cassava, garri, beverages, wrappers and textiles, live animals and other goods moving through the local trading economy. She sourced from farmers, aggregators and people close to production, then moved products toward markets where demand and commercial value were stronger.

She travelled to Aba to sell, and her wider trading activity connected local supply with larger commercial routes including Aba, Onitsha and other markets and communities. She also operated a logistics boat that transported traders, customers, business people, sellers and travellers between communities and commercial destinations.

For Jerry, this was not simply a family story. It was his first practical education in entrepreneurship. He observed purchasing, pricing, customer relationships, selling, storage, movement of goods, negotiation, demand and distribution. At times he remained around the business premises helping with sales and customers; at other times he accompanied her on commercial trips and saw how products moved from source to market.

TRADITIONAL PROCESSING

Before machinery, there was ingenuity.

Sunlight. Firewood heat. Smoke-drying. Practical preservation using the resources available.

TRADITIONAL VALUE ADDITION

Learning how raw commodities become usable products.

Madam Grace also processed and preserved products using local methods rather than industrial machinery. That experience later became part of the founder's understanding of value addition.

01 · SUNLIGHT

Sun-drying and preservation

Products were dried under sunlight as a practical way of reducing moisture and extending usability where appropriate.

02 · FIREWOOD

Heat and smoke-drying

Firewood and controlled heat were also used. Products could be suspended above the fire so heat and smoke helped dry and preserve them.

03 · EXPERIENCE

Understanding the process behind the product

Those early observations gave the founder a practical appreciation that processing is not an abstract idea: it changes how a commodity can be handled, stored, transported, presented and sold.

The HoneyGuide direction is a modern extension of that value-addition principle: move from raw agricultural commodities toward professionally processed, packaged and market-ready products using appropriate technical systems.

HONEYGUIDE

Turning a lifelong lesson into a business platform.

HoneyGuide is the next stage of the same underlying principle: identify opportunity, understand the source, add value, create a product, connect it to a market and grow with discipline.

The first venture is Macdon's Chips, a proposed packaged sweet-potato snack beginning with a Bayelsa-focused market-entry strategy. The business is being developed around sourcing, processing, packaging, quality control, distribution and customer validation.

The long-term ambition is broader than one product. HoneyGuide is intended to become a Nigerian business platform capable of identifying agricultural value opportunities and developing them into commercially useful products. Diversification, however, is deliberately sequenced behind proof of the first operating system.

The founder's credentials are therefore presented as evidence of preparation—not as a substitute for specialist food-processing expertise. HoneyGuide intends to bring in qualified support where specialist competence is required, particularly food safety, regulatory compliance, engineering, accounting, equipment maintenance and other technical functions.
